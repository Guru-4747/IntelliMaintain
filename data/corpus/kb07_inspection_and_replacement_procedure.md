---
title: Bearing Inspection, Dismounting and Replacement Procedure
type: standard operating procedure (project knowledge base)
---

# 1. Safety before any work

Before inspecting or replacing a bearing, the machine must be stopped and isolated using the site lockout/tagout (LOTO) procedure: switch off, isolate all energy sources (electrical, pneumatic, hydraulic, stored mechanical energy), lock and tag the isolation points and verify zero energy by attempting a start. Allow hot bearings and housings to cool. Use the required personal protective equipment such as gloves and safety glasses.

# 2. Pre-removal inspection and documentation

Record the machine identifier, running hours, recent vibration readings, temperature trend and lubrication history. Check for loose foundation bolts, coupling wear and misalignment, because these can produce vibration that resembles bearing problems. Photograph the assembly before dismounting and mark the orientation of the bearing relative to the load zone.

# 3. Dismounting

Use a mechanical or hydraulic puller, or hydraulic (oil injection) methods for large bearings. Apply the dismounting force to the ring with the interference fit. Do not strike the bearing rings or rolling elements directly with a hammer. Keep the removed bearing for failure analysis: do not clean away evidence before inspection.

# 4. Failure analysis of the removed bearing

Inspect the raceways, rolling elements, cage and seals. Note the location and shape of any damage and the load-zone pattern (the running track). Classify the damage according to ISO 15243 failure mode categories: fatigue (subsurface or surface-initiated), wear (abrasive or adhesive), corrosion (moisture, fretting or false brinelling), electrical erosion (excessive current, current leakage/fluting), plastic deformation (overload or indentation from debris) and fracture and cracking. Identifying the failure mode is necessary to remove the root cause; otherwise the new bearing is likely to fail the same way.

# 5. Mounting the new bearing

Check shaft and housing seats for size, roundness and damage. Mount with correct tools: an induction heater for interference fits on the shaft or a mounting sleeve and press, never hammer blows through the rolling elements. Use the correct lubricant and quantity. After mounting, check that the shaft rotates freely, re-align the coupling, and record baseline vibration (RMS, kurtosis, envelope spectrum) after a run-in period so that future measurements can be compared against it.

# 6. Return to service

Remove locks and tags only after all tools are cleared and personnel are informed. Measure vibration and temperature during the first hours of operation and update the maintenance log with the work order number, findings and the root cause.
