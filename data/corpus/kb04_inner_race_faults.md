---
title: Inner Race Faults - Signatures, Causes and Inspection Actions
type: maintenance guideline (project knowledge base)
---

# 1. Vibration signature of an inner race fault

On a machine with a rotating shaft the inner ring rotates with the shaft, so an inner race defect passes through the load zone once per shaft revolution. The rolling elements strike the defect at the ball pass frequency of the inner race (BPFI), and the impact strength is modulated by the load distribution. As a result, the envelope spectrum shows peaks at BPFI and its harmonics surrounded by sidebands spaced at the shaft rotation frequency (1x rpm). The presence of 1x sidebands around BPFI is a key feature that distinguishes inner race faults from outer race faults. Kurtosis is elevated, and the time waveform often shows impacts whose amplitude varies once per revolution.

# 2. Typical root causes

Common causes of inner race damage include: excessive interference fit on the shaft that reduces internal clearance and increases contact stress; insufficient or degraded lubrication; contamination; misalignment that loads the raceway edge; overloading; rolling contact fatigue; and mounting damage such as hammer blows transmitted through the rolling elements, which leave indentations (false or true brinelling) on the inner race.

# 3. Recommended actions when an inner race fault is indicated

1. Confirm the diagnosis: check the envelope spectrum for BPFI harmonics with 1x rpm sidebands and verify the running speed.
2. Increase the monitoring interval frequency and trend kurtosis, RMS and BPFI amplitude; inner race faults often progress faster than outer race faults because the defect passes through the load zone every revolution.
3. Check lubrication condition and relubrication interval.
4. Check shaft fit, alignment and operating load; look for signs of overheating such as discoloration or increased bearing temperature.
5. Schedule replacement at the earliest planned stop; stop immediately if temperature, noise or vibration rises quickly.
6. After removal, inspect the inner ring raceway and shaft seat, document the damage pattern and identify the root cause before reinstalling a new bearing, using correct mounting tools (induction heater or press, never direct hammer blows on the rings).

# 4. Severity guidance

Because the defect travels through the load zone, inner race spalls can grow quickly once started. An increasing BPFI envelope amplitude over successive measurements, rising temperature and a growing number of harmonics are signs that the remaining life is short.
