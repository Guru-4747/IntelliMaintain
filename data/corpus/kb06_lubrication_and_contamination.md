---
title: Bearing Lubrication, Contamination Control and Related Failure Modes
type: maintenance guideline (project knowledge base)

# 1. Role of lubrication

The lubricant separates the rolling elements from the raceways with a thin film, reduces friction and wear, protects against corrosion and, for oil, removes heat. Inadequate lubrication is one of the most frequent causes of premature bearing failure. Symptoms include rising bearing temperature, a rising high-frequency noise floor in the vibration spectrum, increased high-frequency band energy and, in later stages, surface distress (glazing, micro-spalling) on the raceways.

# 2. Relubrication practice

Relubrication intervals depend on bearing type, size, speed, temperature and environment and should be taken from the bearing or machine manufacturer's recommendations. Over-greasing is also harmful: too much grease causes churning, high temperature and seal damage. Use the specified grease type and do not mix incompatible greases. Clean the grease fitting before relubrication to avoid introducing dirt. Record each relubrication in the maintenance log so that vibration changes can be correlated with lubrication events.

# 3. Contamination

Solid contaminants (dust, sand, metal particles from wear or machining) are rolled into the raceways and create dents. Stress concentrations at dent edges start fatigue cracks that grow into spalls. Liquid contaminants such as water reduce lubricant film strength and cause corrosion. Contamination control measures include effective seals and shields, clean handling during mounting, filtered oil systems and closing grease containers.

# 4. Lubricant analysis

Oil or grease samples can be analysed for wear metal particles (iron, chromium for bearing steel), water content and oxidation. A rising concentration of bearing-steel particles combined with increasing vibration kurtosis or envelope amplitude supports a diagnosis of active bearing damage.

# 5. Interpreting vibration after relubrication

If high-frequency vibration and the noise floor fall significantly after relubrication and stay low, the problem was a lubrication deficiency rather than a localized defect. If impacting at a defect frequency persists or returns within days, a localized defect is present and replacement should be planned.
